GRACE trust architecture

Own the stack. Protect the ministry.

GRACE is being reinforced around a simple discipline: use modular foundations we can control, keep sensitive ministry work close, and remove the meter wherever an owned alternative can do the job well. The end state is greater church control without turning a church into an infrastructure company.

End stateAppropriate workloads run on infrastructure GRACE or the church controls
The operating model

Own the stack where it matters.

Open source is not a slogan or a claim that every component is locally operated today. It is the discipline that keeps GRACE modular, portable, and capable of moving with a church over time. The aim is simple: a church should not have to send sensitive work away or pay a new per-task meter when the stack we control can do the job well.

Current

Cloud-tested and supported

GRACE runs in the cloud today so we can test, monitor, secure, and support the product consistently. That is the current delivery model.

Current

Use the strongest tool when it wins

Not every workload belongs on a controlled stack today. Where an outside service is materially stronger on quality, resilience, safety, or support, we use it under clear data terms rather than pretend otherwise.

End state

Dedicated infrastructure when ready

We are building toward operating appropriate workloads on GRACE-owned or customer-controlled, Mac Studio-class hardware, or in the cloud. This is a roadmap direction, not a live deployment option.

What stays true in either model

Trust practices are product features.

The goal is not to sell security as a line item. The controls a church needs should be part of how GRACE works, whether a workload is running in the cloud or later on dedicated infrastructure. The honest bill is operational work: GRACE carries hardware, patching, backups, security response, integrations, and support.

Church data stays scoped

GRACE uses database-level tenant isolation and role-based permissions so people see only the records their role requires.

Access leaves a record

Security-relevant permission denials and access events are recorded in append-only logs for review and accountability.

People remain accountable

Consequential actions and care workflows require clear roles, evidence, and human responsibility. Automation does not erase pastoral judgment.

Data is portable

Our intended operating model is that a church can obtain its records in usable formats and is never trapped by an opaque export process.

Important distinction: greater customer-controlled deployment is an intended end state. It is not presented as a feature already available in the current cloud product.
The GRACE Impact Card

Visible potential. No premature promise.

The Impact Card remains part of GRACE’s long-term model: a possible way for members to direct a share of eligible everyday card economics toward designated ministry initiatives. It is currently a concept under evaluation. GRACE has no executed banking, sponsorship, or program-management agreement for it today, so projected value remains separate from live operating value. Individual member spending is not used for a public or in-app leaderboard.
